# Tharaic — A Fictional Language for a Seafaring Culture

## Phase 1 — Cultural & Linguistic Context

**Who are the speakers?**
A seafaring people — traders, navigators, coastal settlers — living in a Mediterranean-analog world. They are pragmatic and poetic in equal measure: their oral tradition lives in sailor chants, navigation mnemonics, and oral contracts sealed with formulaic speech. Their written tradition covers trade ledgers, sea charts, and temple inscriptions.

**Linguistic ancestry:**
Tharaic descends from a proto-language that absorbed two streams:
- A **Greek-analog branch** → contributed vowel richness, inflectional endings, and poetic vocabulary
- A **Phoenician-analog branch** → contributed VSO word order tendency, trade/mercantile vocabulary, and some consonantal texture

### 2.3 Phonotactics — The "No Cluster" Rule

This is a defining feature of Tharaic. The language breathes. It is made for voices on open water.

**Allowed syllable shapes:** V, CV, VC, CVC

**Rules:**
- Maximum **one consonant** at syllable onset
- Maximum **one consonant** at syllable coda — and only from: **n, s, r, l, m, k**
- No consonant clusters at word start (str-, skr-, phl- etc.)
- No heavy internal clusters (-ntr-, -kst- etc.)
- No *kh* in word-final position
- Words prefer to end in **vowels** or **-n, -s, -r, -l**

**Stress:** Default **penultimate** (second-to-last syllable). Aeolic recessive accent influence.
> *navā* → na-VĀ | *thāros* → THĀ-ros | *elekamen* → e-le-KA-men

## Phase 2.5 — The Consonantal Root System

Every Tharaic word descends from a consonantal root — usually **biconsonantal** (2 consonants) or **triconsonantal** (3 consonants). The root carries the *meaning*; vowels, affixes, and stem patterns carry the *grammar*. Roots are written with hyphens.

## Phase 3 — Grammar

### 3.1 Word Order: VSO — Mandatory

VSO is the only canonical order in all registers. Word order variation is used exclusively for **emphasis**.

> *Nāvei has navos ān navān.* — Sails the-sailor the-ship. → "The sailor sails the ship."

Fronting a noun for emphasis:
> *Ān navān nāvei has navos.* — THE SHIP — the sailor sails it.

---

### 3.2 Gender: Animate vs. Inanimate

| Gender | Applies to | Examples |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Animate (AN)** | People, animals, deities, winds, ships, the sea, stars, fire | *navā, thāros, velos, astros, pyra* |
| **Inanimate (IN)** | Objects, materials, places, concepts, abstractions | *lakon, shamon, taron, hablon* |

Ships, the sea, winds, and stars are animate — these forces have agency. They are spoken to, bargained with, and honored.

---

### 3.3 Noun Paradigms

**Four stem classes:**

| Stem | Gender | NOM.SG marker | Paradigm noun |
|------|--------|--------------|---------------|
| **O-stem** | Animate | *-os* | *thāros* (sea) |
| **A-stem** | Animate | *-ā* | *navā* (ship) |
| **ON-stem** | Inanimate | *-on* | *lakon* (word) |
| **AR-stem** | Inanimate | *-ar* | *pelar* (expanse) |

**The eight cases:**

| Case | Function | Equivalent |
|------|----------|------------|
| **Nominative (NOM)** | Subject of the verb | — |
| **Accusative (ACC)** | Direct object | — |
| **Genitive (GEN)** | Possession, partitive | *of* |
| **Dative (DAT)** | Indirect object, beneficiary | *to, for* |
| **Vocative (VOC)** | Direct address | *O!, Hey!* |
| **Locative (LOC)** | Static location | *in, at, on* |
| **Instrumental (INS)** | Means, accompaniment | *with, by means of* |
| **Ablative (ABL)** | Separation, source | *from, out of, away* |

The Locative, Instrumental, and Ablative are the Phoenician-origin cases — the *three sea cases*: Where are you? How did you get here? Where did you come from?

### 3.4 Construct State — Phoenician Possession

Tharaic has **two ways to express possession**:

**① Genitive case** — Greek-derived, everyday spoken language:
> *ā navā hi bāsili* — the ship of the captain

**② Construct state** — Phoenician-derived, for titles, epithets, sacred formulas, and permanent relationships.

| Stem | Normal NOM.SG | Construct form |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| O-stem | *-os* | *-il* |
| A-stem | *-ā* | *-it* |
| ON-stem | *-on* | *-ul* |
| AR-stem | *-ar* | *-arul* |

Examples:
> *bāsil navā* — "the ship's captain" (permanent role, title)
> *navit mālikos* — "the ruler's ship" (identity, heraldic)
> *lakul thāros* — "the law of the sea" (fixed sacred expression)

**Rule of thumb:** Construct = titles, sacred phrases, inherent identity. Genitive = everyday, temporary, incidental possession.

### 3.6 Verbs

#### Future: particle *sha* + present

> *sha nāvō* — I will sail
> *sha nāvei has navos* — The sailor will sail

#### Narrative past prefix *va-* (Phoenician wayyiqtol-analog)

In oral storytelling and chant, sequential past events are marked with *va-* prefixed to the present-tense form.

> *va-Enāve has velos… va-Enāva has navā… va-Eleka has bāsilos.*
> "The wind rose… the ship moved… the captain spoke."

This form is for **oral narrative and poetry only**. Formal written prose uses the augment past.
