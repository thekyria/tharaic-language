# Syntax & Function Words

[← Overview](../README.md)

## Word Order: VSO — Mandatory

VSO is the only canonical order in all registers. Word order variation is used exclusively for **emphasis**.

> *Νᾱϝει ναϝος ναϝᾱν.* — Sails sailor ship. → "The sailor sails the ship."

Fronting a noun for emphasis:
> *Ναϝᾱν νᾱϝει ναϝος.* — THE SHIP — the sailor sails it.

A **long subject**, such as one with a relative clause, may move to the end of the clause, after the object and other phrases:
> *Φαγει λαχαμον εν λιμελι ενος ϸε-ϸερει σᾱϙιροι.* — Eats bread in harbor-LOC one that-sings storm-DAT. → "He who sings to the storm eats his bread in the harbor."

A verb before a **compound subject** agrees with its first part, as in Hebrew:
> *Εκανε ϳεσει προς-υ κολ γαβυλον και κολ αδαμιμ.* — Was goes-out to-him all region and all people. → "The whole region and all the people were going out to him." (everyday wording; [Mark 1:5](mark-1.md#john-the-immerser-18) has the high-register *εκανε ϳεσοντον*, inanimate like *γαβυλον*)

**Adjectives:** an attributive adjective comes **before** its noun (*καλος ϝελος* "a fair wind"); a predicate adjective follows the subject (*Εσι ϝελος καλος* "The wind is fair"). Numbers behave like attributive adjectives (*ενος ρεμευς* "one rower"). After a noun, a number labels it instead and does not agree: *ͱημερον τρια* "day three" ([Calendar § Dates](calendar.md#dates)).

A few small words always come **before** the verb, in this order:

| Slot | Words |
|------|-------|
| 1. Conjunction or question word | *ιμ* "if", *οτε* "when", *μι* "who", *λαμα* "why"… |
| 2. Question prefix | *ͱα-* |
| 3. Negation, wish or unreal particle | *βαλ*, *μη*, *λυ*, *αν* |
| 4. Future particle | *ϸα* |
| then | verb — subject — object — other phrases |

> *Λαμα βαλ ϸα νᾱϝεις?* — Why not FUT you-sail? → "Why won't you sail?"

Two high-register conjunctions, *γαρ* "for" and *ων* "so", come **second** in their clause instead, as in Greek ([§ High-register clauses](#high-register-clauses)).

## Negation

Tharaic has two negative particles, one from each ancestral stream:

| Particle | Romanized | Used for | Origin |
|----------|-----------|----------|--------|
| *βαλ* | bal | statements and questions about facts | Phoenician *bl* |
| *μη* | mē | commands, wishes, and conditions | Greek *μή* |

> *Βαλ νᾱϝει ναϝος.* — "The sailor does not sail."
> *Βαλ ϸα νᾱϝω.* — "I will not sail."
> *Μη νᾱϝε!* — "Don't sail!"
> *Μη ιδει-νι θᾱρος!* — "May the sea not see me!"

**The narrative past cannot be negated.** A story that needs "and he did not…" switches to the aorist: *ϝα-ϝενει ϝελος… βαλ ενᾱϝε ναϝᾱ* "the wind came… (but) the ship did not sail." The Hebrew *wayyiqtol* behaves the same way.

"No one" and "nothing" are *βαλ … ενος* "not one" (animate) and *βαλ … ενον* (inanimate):

> *Βαλ ιδει ενος ναϝᾱν.* — "No one sees the ship."

### Wishes

A positive wish puts **λυ** "may…, if only…" (Phoenician-analog, cf. Hebrew *lū*) before the present form; its negative is *μη*:

> *Λυ φερει-τα θᾱρος!* — "May the sea carry you!"
> *Μη λαμβανει-τα θᾱρος!* — "May the sea not take you!"

With *we*, *λυ* means "let us": *Λυ αγομεν!* "Let us go!"

## Questions

### Yes/no questions

Prefix **ͱα-** to the first word of the clause (Phoenician/Hebrew *ha-*). Word order does not change.

> *Ͱα-νᾱϝει ναϝος?* — "Does the sailor sail?"
> *Ͱα-βαλ νᾱϝεις?* — "Aren't you sailing?" (expects *yes*)

| Answer | Tharaic | Romanized |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| yes | *ναι* | nai |
| no | *βαλ* | bal |

### Question words

Question words come first in the clause. *Who*, *what* and *why* come from the Phoenician-analog stream; *where*, *when*, *how* and *which* from the Greek-analog stream.

| Tharaic | Romanized | Meaning | Origin |
|---------|-----------|---------|--------|
| *μι* | mi | who, whom | Phoenician *my* |
| *μις* | mis | whose | *μι* + genitive *-ς* |
| *μα* | ma | what | Phoenician *m* |
| *λαμα* | lama | why ("for what") | Phoenician *l-* "for" + *m* |
| *που* | pou | where | Greek-analog *ποῦ* |
| *ποτε* | pote | when | Greek-analog *πότε* |
| *πως* | pōs | how | Greek-analog *πῶς* |
| *ποιος* / *ποιον* | poios / poion | which (AN / IN), declines like *καλος* | Greek-analog *ποῖος* |
| *ποσος* / *ποσον* | posos / poson | how many, how much (AN / IN) | Greek-analog *πόσος* |
| *μαιν* | main | from where, whence | Phoenician-analog, cf. Hebrew *mēʾayin* |

*μι* and *μα* do not decline. Their role comes from the rest of the clause: if a nominative subject follows the verb, *μι* is the object.

> *Μι νᾱϝει?* — "Who sails?"
> *Μι ιδει ναϝος?* — "Whom does the sailor see?" (*ναϝος* is the subject)
> *Μα φερει ναϝᾱ?* — "What does the ship carry?"
> *Που εσι λιμᾱ?* — "Where is the harbor?"

## Relative Clauses

A relative clause follows its noun and begins with the prefix **ϸε-** (Phoenician *š-*, Hebrew *še-*). *ϸε-* never changes form. It attaches to the first word of the clause: normally the verb, but a negation comes first (*ϸε-βαλ ιδει* "who does not see").

When the noun is the **subject** of the relative clause, nothing else is needed. In any other role, a **resumptive pronoun** marks its place, as in Phoenician and Hebrew:

| Role | Example | Literally |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Subject | *ναϝᾱ ϸε-νᾱϝει θᾱρελι* — "the ship that sails on the sea" | ship that-sails sea-LOC |
| Object | *ναϝᾱ ϸε-ιδει-υ ναϝος* — "the ship that the sailor sees" | ship that-sees-it sailor |
| Possessor | *ναϝος ϸε-νᾱϝει ναϝᾱυ* — "the sailor whose ship sails" | sailor that-sails ship-his |

The resumptive pronoun is the object enclitic on the verb (*-υ*, *-α*…) or the possessive enclitic on the noun ([Pronouns & Enclitics](morphology.md#pronouns--enclitics)).

**"The one who…"**: a relative clause without a noun of its own takes *ενος* "one" (animate) or *ενον* (inanimate) as its head:
> *Ϸενει επι συρελι ενος ϸε-βαλ ιδει αστρονας.* — "He who does not watch the stars sleeps on the reef."

In the high register, a [participle phrase](#participle-phrases) usually takes the place of a relative clause.

## Participle phrases

[Participles](morphology.md#participles) are adjectives, so they agree with the noun they describe. A participle with words of its own forms a phrase, and inside it the participle comes first, like the verb of a clause. Everyday speech uses few such phrases. **The high register builds its long sentences with them.**

**1. Describing a noun.** The phrase follows the noun, as a relative clause does:

> *Ιδω ναϝᾱν νᾱϝοντον θᾱρελι.* — I-see ship-ACC sailing-ACC sea-LOC. → "I see the ship sailing on the sea."<br>
> Everyday: *Ιδω ναϝᾱν ϸε-νᾱϝει θᾱρελι.* "I see the ship that sails on the sea."

**2. As a noun**, "the one who…", in place of *ενος ϸε-*:

> *Εσι μακαριος ποιοντος δικαιοσυνᾱν.* — Is blessed doing justice-ACC. → "Blessed is the one who does justice."<br>
> Everyday: *Εσι αϸαρος ενος ϸε-ϙεσει σαδακον.*

**3. Setting the scene.** A participle that agrees with the subject or object tells what happened before (aorist) or at the same time (present). The phrase usually comes before the main clause, which keeps VSO order:

> *Βλεψαντιμ λιμᾱν, ευχαριστησαν ναϝιμ ηλοιμ.* — Having-seen-PL harbor-ACC, gave-thanks sailors gods-DAT. → "When they saw the harbor, the sailors gave thanks to the gods."<br>
> Everyday: *Ϝα-νεβετουσι λιμᾱν ναϝιμ, ϝα-ϳεδουσι ηλοιμ.* "The sailors saw the harbor, and they thanked the gods."

**4. The genitive absolute.** When the participle has a subject of its own, not part of the main clause, both go in the **genitive**, as in Greek:

> *Σᾱϙιρι ϝεναντι, εϸεβε ναϝᾱ.* — Storm-GEN having-come-GEN, turned-back ship. → "When the storm had come, the ship turned back."<br>
> *Ϝελι ϝελοντι, ενᾱϝε ναϝᾱ.* — Wind-GEN blowing-GEN, sailed ship. → "While the wind was blowing, the ship sailed."<br>
> Everyday: *Οτε ενᾱϝε ναϝᾱ, εκανε ϝελει ϝελος.* "When the ship sailed, the wind was blowing."

The genitive absolute belongs to the high register only. Everyday speech uses a clause with *οτε*.

## Conditions

| Tharaic | Romanized | Meaning |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| *ιμ* | im | if (Phoenician *ʾm*) |
| *ιμ μη* | im mē | unless, if not |
| *οτε* | ote | when, whenever |
| *αν* | an | particle of the unreal result, "would" (Greek-analog *ἄν*) |

The condition comes first. An "if" clause is negated with *μη*, never *βαλ*.

The high register says *αι* for *ιμ* and *αι μη* for *ιμ μη* ([§ High-register clauses](#high-register-clauses)).

**Real conditions** use ordinary tenses:
> *Ιμ εσι ϝελος καλος, ϸα νᾱϝομεν.* — "If the wind is fair, we will sail."
> *Ιμ μη ϝενει ϝελος, βαλ ϸα νᾱϝομεν.* — "Unless the wind comes, we will not sail."
> *Οτε ϝενει ϝελος, νᾱϝομεν.* — "Whenever the wind comes, we sail."

**Unreal conditions** put *αν* before the verb of the result clause:
- past aorist in both clauses for the past: "if … had …, would have …"
- imperfective past in both clauses for the present: "if … were …, would …"

> *Ιμ εκανε ϝελος καλος, αν ενᾱϝαμεν.* — "If the wind had been fair, we would have sailed."
> *Ιμ εκανε νᾱϝει ναϝος, αν εκανα νᾱϝω.* — "If the sailor were sailing, I would be sailing."

## Comparison

The standard of comparison goes in the **ablative**, "from", as in the Semitic languages:

> *Εσι ναϝᾱ μεγοτερος καπαναθα.* — Is ship bigger merchant-vessel-ABL. → "The ship is bigger than the merchant vessel."

For a pronoun, the standard is *απο* with an enclitic, like Hebrew *mimmennî* "than I": *γαβαροτερος απο-νι* "mightier than I".

See [Adjectives § Comparison](morphology.md#comparison) for the forms.

## Adverbs

The inanimate singular of an adjective serves as an adverb of manner, as in Greek (*ταχύ*, *πολύ*):

> *Λεκει καλον.* — "He speaks well."
> *Ϝενει ταχον.* — "He comes quickly."

**How many times** is a number with *παϙαμα*, the plural of *παϙαμον* "a time, an occurrence":

> *τρια παϙαμα* — "three times" | *επτα δεχα παϙαμα* — "seventy times"

**Without:** Tharaic has no negative prefix like English *un-* or Greek *ἀ-*. It uses *βιλτι* "without" with the ablative:

> *ζαραϙον βιλτι καρπαθα* — "seed without fruit, fruitless seed" | *βιλτι αμαναθα* — "without faith, unbelieving"

## Prepositions

Prepositions are unstressed and lean on the next word. Each one takes a fixed case, and some change meaning with the case. The cases follow the same logic as the *three sea cases*: **where** (LOC), **where to** (ACC), **where from** (ABL), **with what** (INS).

| Tharaic | Romanized | + Case | Meaning |
| --------- | ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| *εν* | en | LOC | in, within |
| *επι* | epi | LOC | on, upon (rest) |
| | | ACC | onto (motion) |
| *παρα* | para | LOC | beside, alongside |
| | | ACC | to the side of |
| | | ABL | from beside, from someone |
| *περι* | peri | LOC | around (place) |
| | | GEN | about, concerning |
| *ανα* | ana | ACC | up, up along |
| *κατα* | kata | ACC | down, along |
| *προς* | pros | ACC | toward, against |
| *δια* | dia | ACC | through, across |
| | | GEN | because of |
| *απο* | apo | ABL | from, away from |
| *εκ* | ek | ABL | out of |
| *συν* | syn | INS | with, together with |
| *αντι* | anti | GEN | against, opposite, instead of |
| *ͱυπο* | hypo | LOC | under (rest) |
| | | ACC | to under (motion) |
| *ͱυπερ* | hyper | GEN | for, on behalf of; above, more than |
| *αχαρε* | akhare | GEN | after (time); behind (place) |
| *λαπανε* | lapane | GEN | before, in front of, in the presence of |
| *ϙεβερ* | gheber | GEN | beyond, on the other side of |
| *βεν* | ben | GEN | between, among |
| *βαϙαβυρ* | baghabur | GEN | for the sake of |
| *ϙαδι* | ghadi | ACC | until, as far as |
| *βιλτι* | bilti | ABL | without |

The prepositions in the lower part of the table come from the Phoenician-analog stream (*αχαρε*, *λαπανε* "to the face of", *ϙεβερ*, *βεν*, *βαϙαβυρ*, *ϙαδι*, *βιλτι*), except the Greek-analog *ͱυπο* and *ͱυπερ*.

> *εν λιμελι* — "in the harbor" (LOC)
> *προς λιμᾱν* — "toward the harbor" (ACC)
> *απο λιμαθα* — "away from the harbor" (ABL)
> *δια θᾱρον* — "across the sea" (ACC)
> *συν φιλομ* — "with a friend" (INS)
> *περι θᾱρι* — "about the sea" (GEN)

The sea cases also work alone, without a preposition: *θᾱρελι* "on the sea", *ναϝαθα* "from the ship". A preposition adds precision: *επι θᾱρελι* "upon the sea", *εκ ναϝαθα* "out of the ship".

### High-register clauses

The high register joins clauses with Doric conjunctions, and it puts subordinate clauses and participle phrases **before** the main clause, building long Greek-style periods. Inside each clause, VSO order still holds.

| High | Everyday | Meaning | Example |
|------|----------|---------|---------|
| *αι* | *ιμ* | if | *Αι εσι ϝελος καλος, ϸα νᾱϝομεν.* "If the wind is fair, we will sail." |
| *ͱοκα* | *οτε* | when, whenever | *Ͱοκα ϝενει ϝελος, νᾱϝομεν.* "Whenever the wind comes, we sail." |
| *ͱινα* | *λεμαϙαν* | so that, in order that | *Εδιδαξε-νεν διδασκαλος, ͱινα νοομες.* "The teacher taught us so that we would understand." |
| *πριν* | *τερεμ* | before | *Ευχεσθε πριν νᾱϝετε!* "Pray before you sail!" |
| *ͱως* | *κεμω* | as, just as | |
| *επει* | *ͱοτι* | since, because | *ͱοτι* stays for "that" after verbs of saying and knowing |
| *ωστε* | | so that, with the result that; + infinitive, with its subject in the accusative | *ωστε βαλ ϳεκελειν Ϳεϸῡϙον βεϝειν* "so that Jesus could not go in" ([Mark 1:45](mark-1.md#a-man-with-leprosy-4045)) |
| *γαρ* | | for, because (second word) | *Βαλ ϸα νᾱϝομεν· ϝενει γαρ σᾱϙιρος.* "We will not sail, for a storm is coming." |
| *ων* | *λακεν* | so, therefore (second word) | |
| *τοκα* | *αζα* | then, at that time | |
| *ποκα* | | once, at some time; ever | |

These are the *-κα* adverbs and conjunctions of Doric (*ὅκα*, *τόκα*, *πόκα*, for Attic *ὅτε*, *τότε*, *ποτέ*), which mark a speaker as educated as clearly as *ᾱ* for *η* does.

## Demonstratives

| Tharaic | Romanized | Meaning |
| --------- | ----------- | --------- |
| *ζε* | ze | this, these (before a noun; Phoenician *z*) |
| *δεκ* | dek | that, those (before a noun; Aramaic-analog *dēk*) |

> *Ζε αβανον εβενε χᾱριϸος.* — This stone built smith. → "This stone the smith built."

*ζε* and *δεκ* do not change for case, gender or number.
