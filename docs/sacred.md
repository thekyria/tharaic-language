# The Sacred Register

[← Overview](../README.md)

Temple speech, prayers, oaths and inscriptions use a register of their own. It is the same language, but it chooses the older and more Phoenician side of every pair.

*This page describes the older temple style. Temple speech is now a style of the [high register](registers.md), which uses Doric words for theology and the aorist for narrative. The page will be revised to match.*

---

## How it differs from everyday speech

| Feature | Everyday | Sacred | See |
|---------|----------|--------|-----|
| Word forms | *τρια*, *προς*, *ναϝιτ* | chant forms: *τιρια*, *πορος*, *ναϝιτα* | [History § The chant register](history.md#3-the-chant-register) |
| Vocabulary | Greek loans: *σεληνᾱ* moon, *καρδιᾱ* heart, *ακουειν* hear | native and Phoenician words: *ϳαραχᾱ*, *λαβον*, *ϸεμεϙειν* | [Lexicon](lexicon.md) |
| Possession | genitive: *ναϝᾱ βᾱσιλι* | construct: *βᾱσιλ ναϝᾱ* | [Construct state](morphology.md#construct-state--phoenician-possession) |
| Superlative | *-οτατος*: *καλοτατος* | construct with its own plural: *ηλιλ ηλιμ* "god of gods" | [Comparison](morphology.md#comparison) |
| Telling the past | written prose: aorist *ενᾱϝε* | narrative *ϝα-* + present *ϝα-νᾱϝει*, as in oral tales | [Verbs](morphology.md#verbs) |
| Wishes | *λυ* + present | *λυ* + present, often repeated in pairs | [Wishes](syntax.md#wishes) |

## Divine names and epithets

The gods are addressed by construct titles. A title takes its case on its last word: *Βαλιλ ϳαμος* in the nominative, *Βαλιλ ϳαμοι* "to the Lord of the Deep", *Βαλιλ ϳαμε!* "O Lord of the Deep!".

| Tharaic | Romanized | Meaning |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| *Βαλιλ ϳαμος* | Balil yamos | "Lord of the Deep", god of the western sea |
| *Βαλιλ ϸαμον* | Balil shamon | "Lord of Heaven", the sky god (cf. Phoenician *Baʿl Šamēm*) |
| *ηλιλ ηλιμ* | ēlil ēlim | "god of gods", the highest god, whoever is being prayed to |
| *μᾱλικιλ γαλιμ* | mālikil galim | "king of the waves", the storm *σᾱϙιρος* |
| *αμμιτ ναϝιμ* | ammit navim | "Mother of Ships", the harbor and its goddess. *ναϝιμ* also means "sailors" |
| *Μᾱτος* | Mātos | Death, named in curses and proverbs but never prayed to |
| *Μοιρᾱ* | Moirā | Fate, who measures each voyage |
| *Γαδος* | Gados | Luck, invoked by merchants |
| *ϳαραχᾱ* | yarakhā | the moon in sacred speech (everyday *σεληνᾱ*) |

## Formulas

**Invocation** at the start of a voyage:
> *Βαλιλ ϳαμε, μᾱλικιλ γαλιμ — λυ φερεις ναϝᾱν πορος λιμᾱν!*<br>
> Balil yame, mālikil galim — lu phereis navān poros limān!<br>
> Lord-CONSTR deep-VOC, king-CONSTR waves — WISH you-carry ship-ACC toward harbor-ACC!<br>
> "Lord of the Deep, king of the waves, may you carry the ship to the harbor!"

**Offering:**
> *Ζεβεχομεν ζαβαχον Βαλιλ ϳαμοι.*<br>
> Zebekhomen zabakhon Balil yamoi.<br>
> We-sacrifice offering Lord-CONSTR deep-DAT.<br>
> "We offer the sacrifice to the Lord of the Deep."

**Blessing**, given by a priest or by a parent to a child about to sail:
> *Λυ βερεχει-τα Βαλιλ ϸαμον! Λυ φερει-τα θᾱρος!*<br>
> Lu berekhei-ta Balil shamon! Lu pherei-ta thāros!<br>
> WISH blesses-you Lord-CONSTR heaven! WISH carries-you sea!<br>
> "May the Lord of Heaven bless you! May the sea carry you!"

**Burial at sea:** the narrative past, then a wish.
> *Ϝα-λαμβανει-υ θᾱρος, ϝα-φερει-υ ϳαμος. Λυ ϸενει εν ϸαολελι.*<br>
> Va-lambanei-u thāros, va-pherei-u yamos. Lu shenei en shaoleli.<br>
> NARR-takes-him sea, NARR-carries-him deep. WISH sleeps in underworld-LOC.<br>
> "The sea took him, the deep carried him. May he sleep in the underworld."

**Votive inscription**, after the Phoenician formula "because he heard his voice, may he bless him":
> *Ζε αβανον εβενε Χᾱριϸος βενιλ Αδενος Βαλιλ ϳαμοι, ͱοτι εϸεμεϙε κολονυ. Λυ βερεχει-υ!*<br>
> Ze abanon ebene Khārishos benil Adenos Balil yamoi, hoti eshemeghe kolonu. Lu berekhei-u!<br>
> This stone built Kharishos son-CONSTR Adenos Lord-CONSTR deep-DAT, because heard voice-his. WISH blesses-him!<br>
> "This stone Kharishos son of Adenos set up for the Lord of the Deep, because he heard his voice. May he bless him!"

The object *ζε αβανον* is fronted for emphasis, as inscriptions begin with the thing they are written on. *Χᾱριϸος* "smith" and *Αδενος* "lord" are used here as personal names.

For oaths and self-curses, see [Proverbs & Oaths § Oaths](proverbs.md#oaths).
