# History

[← Overview](../README.md)

Tharaic did not always look the way it does today. Most of its irregular forms come from a few regular sound changes and from words borrowed at different times. This page traces them.

**Notation:** \* marks a reconstructed form that is not attested. > means "became". Stress falls on the second-to-last syllable at every stage.

| Stage | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Proto-Tharaic** | Strictly open syllables, no clusters. Roots carry meaning through vowel grades (§1) |
| **Old Tharaic** | Contact with the Greek-analog and Phoenician-analog streams; early loans are fitted to the native syllable shape. Change 1 |
| **Classical Tharaic** | Changes 2–4. The everyday language described in these docs |
| **Chant register** | The language of sailor chants and sacred speech: it keeps forms from before changes 2–3 and breaks up clusters in later loans (§3) |

---

## 1. Root vowel grades

A Proto-Tharaic root changed its vowel to change its role, much as Greek has λέγω "I speak" and λόγος "word", or Arabic has *kataba* "he wrote" and *kātib* "writer". Tharaic keeps three grades:

| Grade | Pattern | Used for | Examples |
|-------|---------|----------|----------|
| **e-grade** | CeC- | plain verbs and ordinary agents | *λεκειν* "to speak", *λεκος* "speaker"; *φερειν*, *θελειν*, *μερειν* |
| **a-grade** | CaC-, CaCC- | things, results, places, and words built on them | *λακον* "word", *λακικος* "verbal"; *ναϝον* "voyage", *ναϝᾱ* "ship"; *ταρον* "depth", *ταριος* "deep"; *μαλκον* "kingdom"; *ϸαμον* "sky", *ϸαμαρ* "name" |
| **ā-grade** | CᾱC-, CᾱCιC- | great actors, permanent roles and forces (like the Semitic active participle *qātil*) | *μᾱλικος* "ruler", *μᾱλικᾱ* "queen", *βᾱσιλος* "captain", *θᾱρος* "the open sea" (\*τᾱρος, see change 1) |

Words derived from another word take that word's grade:

- *ναϝος* "sailor" is a-grade because it is built on *ναϝᾱ* "ship": the ship-person.
- *ϸαμειν* "to name" is a-grade because it is built on *ϸαμαρ* "name".
- *μᾱλικειν* "to rule" and *θᾱρειν* "to cross the sea" are ā-grade because they are built on *μᾱλικος* and *θᾱρος*. *νᾱϝειν* "to sail" points to an old actor noun \*νᾱϝος "voyager" that is otherwise lost.

*ναϝᾱ* "ship" is a-grade, the grade of things, yet it is grammatically animate. Ships were moved into the animate gender by the culture, not by the grammar (see [Morphology](morphology.md#gender-animate-vs-inanimate)).

## 2. Sound changes

### Change 1 — τ became θ before a stressed ᾱ (Old Tharaic)

> \*τᾱρος > *θᾱρος* "the open sea", \*τᾱρειν > *θᾱρειν* "to cross the sea"

This is why the root Τ-Ρ shows both τ and θ: the a-grade *ταρον* "depth" and *ταριος* "deep" have short α and keep τ.

In forms where the stress moves off the ᾱ, the change would not apply: LOC \*τᾱρελι, INS PL \*τᾱρομις. These were levelled to θ by analogy with the rest of the word: *θᾱρελι*, *θᾱρομις*.

### Change 2 — Loss of a short vowel before the stress (Classical Tharaic)

A short **ι, υ or ο** was lost in the syllable just before the stressed one, when it stood between an obstruent (a stop or fricative) and **ρ** or **λ**:

| Older form | Classical form | Meaning |
|------------|----------------|---------|
| \*τιρια | *τρια* | three |
| \*ποροᾱ | *προᾱ* | prow |
| \*βοραδυς, \*βοραδον | *βραδυς*, *βραδον* | slow |
| \*μιζιραχᾱ | *μιζραχᾱ* | east |
| \*πορος | *προς* | toward |

- **Prepositions:** they carry no stress of their own and lean on the next word, so their first vowel always counts as "before the stress". That is how \*πορος became *προς*.
- **Words that were not affected:** short α and ε were not lost, so *ταριος*, *σεληνᾱ* and *καλελι* are unchanged.
- **The result:** everyday speech now allows an **obstruent + ρ/λ** at the start of a syllable, which the older language did not ([Phonotactics](phonology.md#phonotactics--the-no-cluster-rule)).

### Change 3 — Construct forms lost their final vowel (Classical Tharaic)

A noun in the construct state is always said together with the noun that follows it, with no stress of its own. Its old final \*-α was lost:

| Stem | Older | Classical |
|------|-------|-----------|
| O-stem | \*-ιλα | *-ιλ* |
| A-stem | \*-ιτα | *-ιτ* |
| ON-stem | \*-υλα | *-υλ* |
| AR-stem | \*-αρυλα | *-αρυλ* |

The τ of *-ιτ* breaks the coda rule. It survives because a construct form never stands before a pause: *ναϝιτ μᾱλικος* "the ruler's ship" is spoken as one phrase.

### Change 4 — Haplology in the construct

When the O-stem construct ending *-ιλ* follows a stem that already ends in -ιλ, one of the two syllables is dropped:

> \*βᾱσιλ-ιλ > *βᾱσιλ* "captain-of", as in *βᾱσιλ ναϝᾱ* "the ship's captain"

### Later loans

Once change 2 had made obstruent + ρ/λ a normal syllable start, words borrowed after it kept such clusters unchanged:

| Word | Meaning | Source |
|------|---------|--------|
| *αστρος*, *αστρα* | star, constellation | Greek-analog star lore |
| *ͱαβλον* | rope, rigging | Phoenician-analog *ḥbl*, a shipwrights' word |

Two loans still break the everyday rules and are felt as foreign, merchants' words:

| Word | Meaning | Source | Why it is irregular |
|------|---------|--------|---------------------|
| *ξενος* | stranger, guest | Greek-analog *ξένος* | ξ is /ks/, a cluster at the start of the word |
| *επτα* | seven | Greek-analog *ἑπτά* | π is not an allowed coda |

## 3. The chant register

Chant meter wants open, ringing syllables. In chant and sacred speech:

- **Native words** keep the forms from before changes 2 and 3, with their own old vowel.
- **Later loans** have their clusters broken up by a copy of the *following* vowel.

| Everyday | Chant | How |
|----------|-------|-----|
| *τρια* | *τιρια* | old vowel kept |
| *προς* | *πορος* | old vowel kept |
| *προᾱ* | *ποροᾱ* | old vowel kept |
| *βραδυς* | *βοραδυς* | old vowel kept |
| *μιζραχᾱ* | *μιζιραχᾱ* | old vowel kept |
| *ναϝιτ*, *λακυλ* | *ναϝιτα*, *λακυλα* | construct vowel kept |
| *αστρος* | *αστορος* | copy of the following ο |
| *αστρα* | *ασταρα* | copy of the following α |
| *ͱαβλον* | *ͱαβολον* | copy of the following ο |
| *ξενος* | *κεσενος* | copy of the following ε |
| *επτα* | *επατα* | copy of the following α |

So a chant form shows a word's history: *τιρια* has its own ι, while *αστορος* only copies the vowel that comes next.

## 4. Nouns that changed gender

| Word | Today | History |
|------|-------|---------|
| *αστρα* | inanimate, "constellation" | Plural of an old inanimate \*αστρον "star (as an object)", now used as a collective singular. It declines like an ON-stem plural (GEN *αστρων*, LOC *αστρελις*) but takes a singular verb, as Greek neuter plurals do. Compare the animate *αστρος*, the star as a living navigator |
| *ρεμα* | inanimate, "oars, a ship's oarage" | Plural of an old \*ρεμον "oar", used the same way as *αστρα* |
| *ͱυδᾱ* | inanimate, "fresh water" | Once animate, "a living spring". It became inanimate as it came to mean the plain material, but kept its A-stem endings. Adjectives agree in the inanimate: *ͱυδᾱ καλον* "good water" |

## 5. The noun dual

The noun dual ends in **-ιν**, from Proto-Tharaic \*-ιν. The genitive and dative are the exception, with -οιν:

| | O-stem | A-stem | ON-stem | AR-stem |
|--|--------|--------|---------|---------|
| NOM / ACC / VOC | *θᾱριν* | *ναϝιν* | *λακοιν* | *πελαριν* |
| LOC | *θᾱρελιν* | *ναϝελιν* | *λακελιν* | *πελαρελιν* |
| INS | *θᾱρομιν* | *ναϝομιν* | *λακομιν* | *πελαρομιν* |
| ABL | *θᾱραθιν* | *ναϝαθιν* | *λακαθιν* | *πελαραθιν* |

The ON-stem uses its genitive-dative form *λακοιν* for all the direct cases too, a common merger in inanimate nouns.

*Earlier versions of this grammar wrote the O- and A-stem dual as -ειν and the instrumental dual as -ομεν. Those forms clashed with the infinitive (θᾱρειν "to cross the sea") and the first-person plural verb (νᾱϝομεν "we sail"), and are replaced by -ιν and -ομιν.*
