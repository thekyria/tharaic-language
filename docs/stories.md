# Chants & Stories

Each line gives the Tharaic, its romanization, a word-by-word gloss, and a translation. Glosses use the case labels of the [noun paradigms](morphology.md#noun-paradigms): NARR is the narrative past *ϝα-*, WISH the particle *λυ*, CONSTR the construct state.

## Song of the Rowers

*Ϸαρον ναϝων* "Song of the Sailors" is a rowing chant. Like all chant, it uses [chant forms](history.md#3-the-chant-register): *μιζιραχᾱ* for everyday *μιζραχᾱ*, *ασταρα* for *αστρα*, *πορος* for *προς*, and the full construct ending *-ιτα*. The refrain marks the stroke of the oars.

> *Ερεσσετε, ναϝιμ! Ερεσσετε!*<br>
> Eressete, navim! Eressete!<br>
> Row-PL, sailors-VOC! Row-PL!<br>
> "Row, sailors! Row!"

> *Ϝενει ϝελος απο μιζιραχαθα,*<br>
> Venei velos apo mizirakhatha,<br>
> Comes wind from east-ABL,<br>
> "The wind comes from the east,"

> *ϝενει γαλος, ϝενει σᾱϙιρος.*<br>
> venei galos, venei sāghiros.<br>
> comes wave, comes storm.<br>
> "the wave comes, the storm comes."

> *Ερεσσετε, ναϝιμ! Ερεσσετε!*<br>
> Eressete, navim! Eressete!<br>
> Row-PL, sailors-VOC! Row-PL!<br>
> "Row, sailors! Row!"

> *Φερει-νεν θᾱρος, βαλ λαμβανει-νεν;*<br>
> Pherei-nen thāros, bal lambanei-nen;<br>
> Carries-us sea, not takes-us;<br>
> "The sea carries us, it does not take us;"

> *ιδει-νεν ασταρα εν ϸαμελι.*<br>
> idei-nen astara en shameli.<br>
> sees-us constellation in sky-LOC.<br>
> "the stars in the sky are watching us."

> *Ερεσσετε, ναϝιμ! Ερεσσετε!*<br>
> Eressete, navim! Eressete!<br>
> Row-PL, sailors-VOC! Row-PL!<br>
> "Row, sailors! Row!"

> *Λυ φερει-νεν ναϝᾱ πορος λιμᾱν, πορος αμμιτα ναϝᾱνας!*<br>
> Lu pherei-nen navā poros limān, poros ammita navānas!<br>
> WISH carries-us ship toward harbor-ACC, toward mother-CONSTR ships-ACC!<br>
> "May the ship carry us toward the harbor, toward the Mother of Ships!"

Notes:
- **Agreement:** *ασταρα* "the constellation" is a collective, so it takes a singular verb ([History § Nouns that changed gender](history.md#4-nouns-that-changed-gender)).
- ***αμμιτα ναϝᾱνας*:** the chant form of *αμμιτ ναϝιμ* "Mother of Ships", a title of the harbor. As a construct phrase it takes its case, here the accusative after *πορος*, on its last word.
- ***ναϝιμ*:** the plural of both *ναϝος* "sailor" and *ναϝᾱ* "ship". Sailors like the double meaning: in this chant the Mother of Ships is the mother of sailors too.

## The Boy and the Storm

A harbor tale, told with the narrative past *ϝα-* + present. The scene is set with the imperfective past *εκανα* + present; the one negated event switches to the aorist, because *ϝα-* cannot be negated.

> *Εκανε ταρϸιϸᾱ εν λιμελι Συρου.*<br>
> Ekane tarshishā en limeli Surou.<br>
> Was great-ship in harbor-LOC Tyre-GEN.<br>
> "There was a great ship in the harbor of Tyre."

> *Εκανε φερει φαρον κασαφου και ϳαινου.*<br>
> Ekane pherei pharon kasaphou kai yainou.<br>
> Was carries cargo silver-GEN and wine-GEN.<br>
> "It was carrying a cargo of silver and wine."

> *Ϝα-νᾱϝει ταρϸιϸᾱ δια θᾱρον προς μᾱραβᾱν.*<br>
> Va-nāvei tarshishā dia thāron pros mārabān.<br>
> NARR-sails great-ship across sea-ACC toward west-ACC.<br>
> "The ship sailed across the sea toward the west."

> *Ϝα-ϝενει σᾱϙιρος απο μιζραχαθα, ϝα-ϝενουσι μεγιμ γαλιμ.*<br>
> Va-venei sāghiros apo mizrakhatha, va-venousi megim galim.<br>
> NARR-comes storm from east-ABL, NARR-come great-PL waves.<br>
> "A storm came from the east, and great waves came."

> *Ϝα-λεκει βᾱσιλος: «Ϸελεκετε φαρον κατα θᾱρον!»*<br>
> Va-lekei bāsilos: «Shelekete pharon kata thāron!»<br>
> NARR-says captain: "Throw-PL cargo down sea-ACC!"<br>
> "The captain said: 'Throw the cargo into the sea!'"

> *Αλλα ϝα-λεκει ϳελεδος: «Μη ϸελεκετε! Ϸα ϸερω σᾱϙιροι.»*<br>
> Alla va-lekei yeledos: «Mē shelekete! Sha sherō sāghiroi.»<br>
> But NARR-says boy: "NEG throw-PL! FUT I-sing storm-DAT."<br>
> "But the boy said: 'Don't throw it! I will sing to the storm.'"

> *Ϝα-γελουσι ναϝιμ, αλλα ϝα-ϸερει ϳελεδος:*<br>
> Va-gelousi navim, alla va-sherei yeledos:<br>
> NARR-laugh sailors, but NARR-sings boy:<br>
> "The sailors laughed, but the boy sang:"

> *«Σᾱϙιρε, μᾱλικιλ γαλιμ, ϸενε! Ϸενε, και λυ ϝενει καλος ϝελος!»*<br>
> «Sāghire, mālikil galim, shene! Shene, kai lu venei kalos velos!»<br>
> "Storm-VOC, king-CONSTR waves, sleep! Sleep, and WISH comes fair wind!"<br>
> "'O Storm, king of the waves, sleep! Sleep, and may a fair wind come!'"

> *Ϝα-ϸενει σᾱϙιρος, ϝα-ϸενουσι γαλιμ.*<br>
> Va-shenei sāghiros, va-shenousi galim.<br>
> NARR-sleeps storm, NARR-sleep waves.<br>
> "The storm slept, and the waves slept."

> *Ϝα-φερει καλος ϝελος ταρϸιϸᾱν προς λιμᾱν, και βαλ εϸελεκαν ναϝιμ φαρον.*<br>
> Va-pherei kalos velos tarshishān pros limān, kai bal eshelekan navim pharon.<br>
> NARR-carries fair wind great-ship-ACC toward harbor-ACC, and not threw sailors cargo.<br>
> "A fair wind carried the ship to harbor, and the sailors never threw the cargo away."

> *Ϝα-λεκει βᾱσιλος: «Ϸα κανεις ναϝος, ϳελεδε.»*<br>
> Va-lekei bāsilos: «Sha kaneis navos, yelede.»<br>
> NARR-says captain: "FUT you-be sailor, boy-VOC."<br>
> "The captain said: 'You will be a sailor, boy.'"

The tale ends with the proverb it explains:

> *Φαγει λαχαμον εν λιμελι ενος ϸε-ϸερει σᾱϙιροι.*<br>
> Phagei lakhamon en limeli enos she-sherei sāghiroi.<br>
> Eats bread in harbor-LOC one that-sings storm-DAT.<br>
> "He who sings to the storm eats his bread in the harbor."
