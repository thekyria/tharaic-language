# Phonology & Script

[← Overview](../README.md)

Tharaic is written in the **Greek alphabet**, extended with a few archaic and auxiliary letters for sounds that Classical Greek lacks:

| Letter | Name | Sound | Origin |
|--------|------|-------|--------|
| **Ϝ ϝ** | digamma | /v/ | Archaic Greek (Aeolic) |
| **Ͱ ͱ** | heta | /h/ | Archaic Greek; used instead of the rough breathing so /h/ can appear inside words |
| **Ϳ ϳ** | yot | /j/ | Greek-alphabet linguistic convention |
| **Ϸ ϸ** | sho | /ʃ/ | Bactrian Greek script |
| **Ϙ ϙ** | koppa | /ɣ/ | Archaic Greek, from Phoenician qoph |

No accent marks are written: stress is predictable (see [Phonotactics](#phonotactics--the-no-cluster-rule)). A final sigma is written **ς**. The Latin romanization is given alongside each letter below for reference.

## Phonotactics — The "No Cluster" Rule

This is a defining feature of Tharaic. The language breathes. It is made for voices on open water.

**Allowed syllable shapes:** V, CV, VC, CVC

**Rules:**
- Maximum **one consonant** at syllable onset
- Maximum **one consonant** at syllable coda — and only from: **ν, σ, ρ, λ, μ, κ**
- No consonant clusters at word start (στρ-, σκρ-, φλ- etc.)
- No heavy internal clusters (-ντρ-, -κστ- etc.)
- No *χ* in word-final position
- Words prefer to end in **vowels** or **-ν, -ς, -ρ, -λ**

These rules hold strictly in **chant** and in the older language. **Everyday speech** has relaxed them in three ways, each with a history ([History § Sound changes](history.md#2-sound-changes)):

| Relaxation | Examples | Origin |
|------------|----------|--------|
| A syllable may start with an **obstruent + ρ/λ** | *τρια*, *προς*, *βραδυς*, *μιζραχᾱ*, *αστρος*, *ͱαβλον* | Loss of short vowels before the stress, then loans that fit the new pattern |
| The construct ending **-ιτ** ends in τ | *ναϝιτ μᾱλικος* | Loss of the final vowel; a construct form never ends a phrase |
| Two unassimilated loans | *ξενος*, *επτα* | Merchants' words from the Greek-analog stream |

No register allows a three-consonant onset (στρ-, σκρ-). Inside a word, everyday speech allows at most a coda consonant followed by obstruent + ρ/λ (*ασ.τρος*); clusters like -κστ- stay excluded.

**Stress:** Default **penultimate** (second-to-last syllable). Aeolic recessive accent influence.
> *ναϝᾱ* → να-ϜᾹ | *θᾱρος* → ΘᾹ-ρος | *ελεκαμεν* → ε-λε-ΚΑ-μεν
