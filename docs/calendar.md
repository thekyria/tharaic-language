# Calendar & Star Lore

[← Overview](../README.md)

A seafaring people keeps time by the sea and the stars. The Tharaic year is **solar**: it begins when a star group rises, not with a new moon. Only the word for month, *ϳαραχον*, built on the moon root Ϳ-Ρ-Χ, remembers an older lunar count.

The star dates below are approximate, for a Mediterranean latitude in antiquity.

---

## The year

| Part | Tharaic | Romanized | Length |
|------|---------|-----------|--------|
| The Open Sea | *Παταχον* | Patakhon | months 1–6, the sailing half |
| The Closed Sea | *Σαγαρον* | Sagaron | months 7–12, when no ship leaves harbor by law |
| The Days of Memory | *ͱημερα ζακαρου* | hēmera zakarou | 5 days (sometimes 6) at the end of the year |

- **Length:** 12 months of 30 days, plus the Days of Memory, makes 365 days, as in the Egyptian civil year.
- **New Year:** the first day of the Open Sea, the morning the **Doves** (the Pleiades) are first seen rising before dawn, around the middle of May.
- **Closing:** the Doves set at dawn around the middle of November, which ends the last month of the Open Sea. Hesiod gives the Greek sailor the same signal for hauling his ship ashore.
- **Keeping in step:** if, on the last Day of Memory, the priests have not seen the Doves, they add a sixth day. That happens about one year in four, so the calendar never drifts from the stars.

> *Ϝενουσι Ϳωνιμ — πετεχεται θᾱρος.*<br>
> Venousi Yōnim — petekhetai thāros.<br>
> Come Doves — opens-itself sea.<br>
> "The Doves come: the sea opens."

> *Δυνουσι Ϳωνιμ — σεγερεται θᾱρος.*<br>
> Dynousi Yōnim — segeretai thāros.<br>
> Set Doves — closes-itself sea.<br>
> "The Doves set: the sea closes."

The sea "opens itself" and "closes itself": the verbs are in the [middle voice](morphology.md#middle-voice).

## The months

A month's full name is a [construct phrase](morphology.md#construct-state--phoenician-possession), *ϳαραχυλ* "month of" + a noun. Everyday speech uses the noun alone.

| No. | Full name | Romanized | Meaning | About | What happens |
|-----|-----------|-----------|---------|-------|--------------|
| | **The Open Sea** | | | | |
| 1 | *ϳαραχυλ Ϳωνιμ* | yarakhul Yōnim | of the Doves | May–June | the Doves rise; the first voyages |
| 2 | *ϳαραχυλ γαληνᾱ* | yarakhul galēnā | of Calm | June–July | the calmest sea, the longest days |
| 3 | *ϳαραχυλ Κελεβος* | yarakhul Kelebos | of the Dog | July–August | the Dog (Sirius) rises; the hottest weeks |
| 4 | *ϳαραχυλ ϳαινον* | yarakhul yainon | of Wine | August–September | the vintage |
| 5 | *ϳαραχυλ Ϸεμερος* | yarakhul Shemeros | of the Watchman | September–October | the Watchman (Arcturus) rises and warns: sail for home |
| 6 | *ϳαραχυλ ναϝιμ* | yarakhul navim | of the Ships | October–November | ships come home and are hauled ashore; the Doves set |
| | **The Closed Sea** | | | | |
| 7 | *ϳαραχυλ σᾱϙιρος* | yarakhul sāghiros | of the Storm | November–December | the first winter storms |
| 8 | *ϳαραχυλ λαιλον* | yarakhul lailon | of Night | December–January | the longest nights |
| 9 | *ϳαραχυλ ϸαλγον* | yarakhul shalgon | of Snow | January–February | snow on the mountains |
| 10 | *ϳαραχυλ ματαρ* | yarakhul matar | of Rain | February–March | rains; small boats fish near the shore |
| 11 | *ϳαραχυλ μαναϝαρ* | yarakhul manavar | of the Shipyard | March–April | hulls are repaired and new keels laid |
| 12 | *ϳαραχυλ Γᾱβιρος* | yarakhul Gābiros | of the Hero | April–May | the Hero (Orion) sinks into the evening twilight; crews are hired |
| | *ͱημερα ζακαρου* | hēmera zakarou | Days of Memory | mid-May | five days for those the sea has taken |

The law closes the sea for the whole Closed Sea. In practice, coasters make short hops along the shore in the months of Rain and the Shipyard.

## Dates

- **"In" a month** is the locative. Because a construct phrase takes its case on its last word, "in the month of the Doves" is *ϳαραχυλ Ϳωνελις*, with the locative plural on *ϳωνιμ*.
- **Numbering:** a number placed **after** a noun labels it, and does not agree: *ͱημερον τρια* "day three, the third day".
- **Compound numbers:** larger units come first, and the last part is joined with *και*. A multiplier goes before *δεχα* or *ͱεχατον*: *δεϝα δεχα* "twenty", *τρια ͱεχατον δεχα και δεϝα* "three hundred and twelve".

> *Ͱημερελι τρια ϳαραχυλ γαληνις.*<br>
> Hēmereli tria yarakhul galēnis.<br>
> Day-LOC three month-CONSTR calm-GEN.<br>
> "On the third day of the month of Calm."

## Counting years

**Everyday dating** names the year after its two judges, *ϸᾱφιτιμ*, who hold office for one year, as the suffetes of Carthage did:

> *Ενιαυτελι ϸᾱφιτων Αδενι και Χᾱριϸι.*<br>
> Eniauteli shāphitōn Adeni kai Khārishi.<br>
> Year-LOC judges-GEN Adenos-GEN and Kharishos-GEN.<br>
> "In the year of the judges Adenos and Kharishos."

**Temple dating** counts years from the legendary founding voyage, *ναϝυλ ραϸιτον* "the Voyage of the Beginning":

> *Ενιαυτελι τρια ͱεχατον δεχα και δεϝα απο ναϝυλ ραϸιταθα.*<br>
> Eniauteli tria hekhaton dekha kai deva apo navul rashitatha.<br>
> Year-LOC three hundred ten and two from voyage-CONSTR beginning-ABL.<br>
> "In the year 312 from the Voyage of the Beginning."

## The week

The week has seven days, running on without regard to the months. Each day belongs to one of the seven lights that move against the stars: the Sun, the Moon, and the five planets, which sailors call **the Sailors**, *ναϝιμ*. A day's full name is *ͱημερυλ* "day of" + the light; everyday speech uses the light's name alone.

| Day | Light | Romanized | Meaning | Planet |
|-----|-------|-----------|---------|--------|
| 1 | *ͱηλιᾱ* | hēliā | the Sun | — |
| 2 | *ϳαραχᾱ* | yarakhā | the Moon | — |
| 3 | *Πυρρος* | Pyrros | the Red One | Mars |
| 4 | *Ταχυς* | Takhys | the Swift One | Mercury |
| 5 | *Μᾱλικος* | Mālikos | the King | Jupiter |
| 6 | *Ϸαχαρ* | Shakhar | Dawn | Venus |
| 7 | *Βραδυς* | Bradys | the Slow One | Saturn |

- **The seventh day** is the day of rest, also called *ϸαβατον* "the ceasing" (Phoenician-analog, cf. Hebrew *šabbāt*). The order is the planetary week of the Hellenistic world, so the rest day falls on Saturn's day, as Saturday does.
- **Venus** is *Ϸαχαρ* "Dawn" as the morning star and *Ϙερεβον* "Dusk" as the evening star. Pilots know they are the same light.

## Time of day

The day runs from sunrise to sunrise. Sailors divide the night into three **watches**, *φυλακα*:

| Part | Tharaic | Romanized | When |
|------|---------|-----------|------|
| Dawn | *ϸαχαρ* | shakhar | first light |
| Midday | *μεσον ͱημερου* | meson hēmerou | the sun in the south, *δᾱρομος* |
| Dusk | *ϙερεβον* | gherebon | sunset |
| Evening watch | *φυλακον ϙερεβου* | phylakon gherebou | from sunset to about four hours after |
| Middle watch | *μεσον φυλακον* | meson phylakon | around midnight |
| Dawn watch | *φυλακον ϸαχαρι* | phylakon shakhari | until first light |

At sea, the watches are timed by the **Little Boat** (the Little Bear) as it turns around the Nail of Heaven.

> *Μεσελι φυλακελι ϝενει βᾱσιλος επι προᾱν.*<br>
> Meseli phylakeli venei bāsilos epi proān.<br>
> Middle-LOC watch-LOC comes captain onto prow-ACC.<br>
> "In the middle watch the captain comes up to the prow."

Words for days:

| Tharaic | Romanized | Meaning |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| *ͱαϳομ* | hayom | today (cf. Hebrew *hayyôm*) |
| *μαχαρ* | makhar | tomorrow (cf. Hebrew *māḥār*) |
| *αμες* | ames | yesterday, last night (cf. Hebrew *ʾemeš*) |

## Festivals

Festivals are named with *χαγυλ* "festival of", the construct form of *χαγον*.

| Festival | Romanized | When | What is done |
|----------|-----------|------|--------------|
| *ͱημερα ζακαρου* | hēmera zakarou | the Days of Memory, the last 5 or 6 days of the year | no trade and no sailing; lamps are set floating on the harbor for those the sea has taken |
| *χαγυλ παταχον* | khagul patakhon | New Year, the morning the Doves rise | ships are launched, an offering goes to the Lord of the Deep, and every crew is blessed |
| *χαγυλ ϳαινον* | khagul yainon | the full moon of the month of Wine | the vintage; the new wine is drunk, and no one sails |
| *χαγυλ σαγαρον* | khagul sagaron | the end of the month of the Ships, when the Doves set | ships are hauled up, and the oars hung in the sanctuary until spring |
| *χαγυλ αρον* | khagul aron | the longest night, in the month of Night | the Festival of Light: fires on every headland until dawn |

What is said:

> *Ζεκερομεν ναϝιμ ϸε-ελαμβανε-ιμ θᾱρος.*<br>
> Zekeromen navim she-elambane-im thāros.<br>
> We-remember sailors that-took-them sea.<br>
> "We remember the sailors whom the sea took." (Days of Memory)

> *Λυ φερει-τα θᾱρος, λυ φερει-τα Βαλιλ ϳαμος!*<br>
> Lu pherei-ta thāros, lu pherei-ta Balil yamos!<br>
> WISH carries-you sea, WISH carries-you Lord-CONSTR deep!<br>
> "May the sea carry you, may the Lord of the Deep carry you!" (New Year, at each launch)

> *Ϸετομεν ϳαινον, και βαλ ϸα νᾱϝομεν!*<br>
> Shetomen yainon, kai bal sha nāvomen!<br>
> We-drink wine, and not FUT we-sail!<br>
> "We drink wine, and we will not sail!" (Festival of Wine)

> *Σεγερεται θᾱρος. Ϸενε, ναϝε!*<br>
> Segeretai thāros. Shene, nave!<br>
> Closes-itself sea. Sleep, ship-VOC!<br>
> "The sea closes. Sleep, ship!" (Closing)

> *Ϝενει αρον!*<br>
> Venei aron!<br>
> Comes light!<br>
> "The light is coming!" (Festival of Light, shouted at first dawn)

## Star lore

| Tharaic | Romanized | Meaning | Stars | Role |
|---------|-----------|---------|-------|------|
| *Ϳωνιμ* | Yōnim | the Doves | the Pleiades | they open and close the sea. The Greek name *Πλειάδες* was linked to *πέλειαι* "doves" |
| *Ματαρα* | Matara | the Rains | the Hyades | their autumn setting brings the rains. The Greek *Ὑάδες* are likewise "the rainy ones" |
| *Κελεβος* | Kelebos | the Dog | Sirius | it rises at the height of summer |
| *Γᾱβιρος* | Gābiros | the Hero | Orion | the winter sky; his spring setting means crews are hired (cf. Arabic *al-Jabbār* "the giant") |
| *Ϸεμερος* | Shemeros | the Watchman | Arcturus | his autumn rising warns ships home (cf. Greek *Ἀρκτοῦρος* "bear-guard") |
| *Ταρϸιϸᾱ* | Tarshishā | the Great Ship | the Great Bear | points the way to the Little Boat |
| *Ναϝιον* | Navion | the Little Boat | the Little Bear | the pilot's constellation; it turns around the Nail. The Greeks called the Little Bear *Φοινίκη*, after the Phoenicians who steered by it |
| *Ͱηλυλ ϸαμον* | Hēlul shamon | the Nail of Heaven | the star nearest the pole | it hardly moves; the whole sky is hung on it |
| *Δαρακυλ ηλιμ* | Darakul ēlim | the Road of the Gods | the Milky Way | the gods walk it; the dead at sea follow it |
| *Ναϝιμ* | Navim | the Sailors | the planets | the five lights that wander; see [The week](#the-week) |

> *Ιδε Ͱηλυλ ϸαμον, και βαλ ϸα αγεις επι συρον.*<br>
> Ide Hēlul shamon, kai bal sha ageis epi suron.<br>
> See nail-CONSTR heaven, and not FUT you-go onto reef-ACC.<br>
> "Watch the Nail of Heaven, and you will not go onto the reef." (a pilot's saying)
