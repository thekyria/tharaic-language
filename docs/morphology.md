## The Consonantal Root System

Every Tharaic word descends from a consonantal root — usually **biconsonantal** (2 consonants) or **triconsonantal** (3 consonants). The root carries the *meaning*; vowels, affixes, and stem patterns carry the *grammar*. Roots are written with hyphens.

The root vowel shows the word's role: **e-grade** for plain verbs and agents (*λεκειν*, *λεκος*), **a-grade** for things and results (*λακον*, *ταρον*), and **ā-grade** for great actors and permanent roles (*μᾱλικος*, *θᾱρος*). See [History § Root vowel grades](history.md#1-root-vowel-grades).

## Gender: Animate vs. Inanimate

| Gender | Applies to | Examples |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Animate (AN)** | People, animals, deities, winds, ships, the sea, stars, fire | *ναϝᾱ, θᾱρος, ϝελος, αστρος, πυρα*<br>navā, thāros, velos, astros, pyra |
| **Inanimate (IN)** | Objects, materials, places, concepts, abstractions | *λακον, ϸαμον, ταρον, ͱαβλον*<br>lakon, shamon, taron, hablon |

Ships, the sea, winds, and stars are animate — these forces have agency. They are spoken to, bargained with, and honored.

A few nouns have endings that don't match their gender, because they changed gender over time: *αστρα* "constellation" and *ρεμα* "oars" are old plurals used as collectives, and *ͱυδᾱ* "fresh water" keeps the A-stem endings of a once-animate noun. See [History § Nouns that changed gender](history.md#4-nouns-that-changed-gender).

## Noun Paradigms

**Four stem classes:**

| Stem | Gender | NOM.SG marker | Paradigm noun |
|------|--------|--------------|---------------|
| **O-stem** | Animate | *-ος*<br>-os | *θᾱρος* (sea)<br>thāros |
| **A-stem** | Animate | *-ᾱ*<br>-ā | *ναϝᾱ* (ship)<br>navā |
| **ON-stem** | Inanimate | *-ον*<br>-on | *λακον* (word)<br>lakon |
| **AR-stem** | Inanimate | *-αρ*<br>-ar | *πελαρ* (expanse)<br>pelar |

**The eight cases:**

| Case | Function | Equivalent |
|------|----------|------------|
| **Nominative (NOM)** | Subject of the verb | — |
| **Accusative (ACC)** | Direct object | — |
| **Genitive (GEN)** | Possession, partitive | *of* |
| **Dative (DAT)** | Indirect object, beneficiary | *to, for* |
| **Vocative (VOC)** | Direct address | *O!, Hey!* |
| **Locative (LOC)** | Static location | *in, at, on* |
| **Instrumental (INS)** | Means, accompaniment | *with, by means of* |
| **Ablative (ABL)** | Separation, source | *from, out of, away* |

The Locative, Instrumental, and Ablative are the Phoenician-origin cases — the *three sea cases*: Where are you? How did you get here? Where did you come from?

The dual ends in **-ιν** (instrumental **-ομιν**); the genitive and dative dual end in -οιν. See [History § The noun dual](history.md#5-the-noun-dual).

- A Doric word that has passed into everyday speech takes the ordinary Tharaic endings: *ισχολᾱ*, GEN *ισχολις*.

## Construct State — Phoenician Possession

Tharaic has **two ways to express possession**:

**① Genitive case** — Greek-derived, everyday spoken language:
> *ναϝᾱ βᾱσιλι* — the ship of the captain

**② Construct state** — Phoenician-derived, for titles, epithets, sacred formulas, and permanent relationships.

| Stem | Normal NOM.SG | Construct form |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| O-stem | *-ος*<br>-os | *-ιλ*<br>-il |
| A-stem | *-ᾱ*<br>-ā | *-ιτ*<br>-it |
| ON-stem | *-ον*<br>-on | *-υλ*<br>-ul |
| AR-stem | *-αρ*<br>-ar | *-αρυλ*<br>-arul |

Examples:
> *βᾱσιλ ναϝᾱ* — "the ship's captain" (permanent role, title)
> *ναϝιτ μᾱλικος* — "the ruler's ship" (identity, heraldic)
> *λακυλ θᾱρος* — "the law of the sea" (fixed sacred expression)

**Rule of thumb:** Construct = titles, sacred phrases, inherent identity. Genitive = everyday, temporary, incidental possession.

**In the high register** the genitive is used for all of these, and the construct survives only in **divine names and fixed titles** (*Βαλιλ ϳαμος*, *ηλιλ ηλιμ*) and in the "son of" of personal names (*Χᾱριϸος βενιλ Αδενος*). Everyday *μαλκυλ ϸαμον* "the kingdom of heaven" is *βασιλειᾱ ωρανω* in the high register ([Registers § Grammar](registers.md#grammar)).

**Notes:**
- A construct form is always said together with the noun after it and never ends a phrase. That is why *-ιτ* can end in τ, which is otherwise not allowed at the end of a syllable.
- Stems that already end in -ιλ drop one syllable: *βᾱσιλος* → *βᾱσιλ*, not \*βᾱσιλιλ.
- Chant keeps the older endings *-ιλα, -ιτα, -υλα, -αρυλα*.
- A construct phrase acts as one unit, and **its case is marked on its last word**: *Ζεβεχομεν ζαβαχον βαλιλ ϳαμοι* "We offer the sacrifice to the Lord of the Deep" (dative on *ϳαμος*); addressing him, *Βαλιλ ϳαμε!* (vocative).

See [History § Change 3](history.md#change-3--construct-forms-lost-their-final-vowel-classical-tharaic).

| 1pl | *ανεν*<br>anen |  |  |
| 2pl | *ατεμ*<br>atem |  |  |
| 3pl AN | *ͱῡιμ*<br>hūim |  |  |
| 3pl IN | *ͱιομ*<br>hiom |  |  |

*ανι* and *ατα* are modeled on Phoenician *ʾnk* (I) and *ʾt* (you).

**Enclitic object pronouns — attach to verbs:**

| Person | Enclitic | Person | Enclitic |
|--------|----------|--------|----------|
| 1sg | *-νι*<br>-ni | 1pl | *-νεν*<br>-nen |
| 2sg | *-τα*<br>-ta | 2pl | *-τεμ*<br>-tem |
| 3sg AN | *-υ*<br>-u | 3pl AN | *-ιμ*<br>-im |
| 3sg IN | *-α*<br>-a | 3pl IN | *-ομ*<br>-om |
| 1du | *-ναιν*<br>-nain | 3du AN | *-αιν*<br>-ain |

> *Ιδει-νι θᾱρος.* — "The sea sees me."
> *Φερω-τα ανι.* — "I carry you."

Object enclitics also stand for:
- an **indirect object**: *λεκει-υ* "he says to him", *Δωε-νεν!* "Give us!"
- a pronoun **after a preposition**, as Phoenician and Hebrew attach suffixes to prepositions: *προς-υ* "to him", *εκ-υ* "out of him", *αχαρε-νι* "after me", *εν-τα* "in you"
- the owner with *εσι* "there is": *Εσι-υ ναϝᾱ.* "He has a ship." (compare *Εσι ναϝᾱ βᾱσιλοι* "The captain has a ship")

**Enclitic possessive pronouns — attach to nouns:**

| Person | Enclitic | Person | Enclitic |
|--------|----------|--------|----------|
| 1sg | *-ι*<br>-i | 1pl | *-εν*<br>-en |
| 2sg | *-κα*<br>-ka | 2pl | *-κεμ*<br>-kem |
| 3sg AN | *-υ*<br>-u | 3pl AN | *-ιμ*<br>-im |
| 3sg IN | *-α*<br>-a | 3pl IN | *-ομ*<br>-om |

> *ναϝᾱι* — my ship | *ναϝᾱκα* — your ship | *ναϝᾱυ* — his/her ship

The enclitic follows the case ending: *κολονυ* "his voice" (ACC *κολον* + *-υ*).

### Reflexive: oneself

Tharaic has no reflexive pronoun. When the middle voice is not enough, "oneself" is *νεφεϸᾱ* "soul" with a possessive enclitic, as in Hebrew *nap̄šô* "his soul, himself":

> *Ϳεϸεϙε νεφεϸᾱνκα!* — Save soul-ACC-your! → "Save yourself!"
> *Εͱεβει ϸεκενον κεμω νεφεϸᾱνυ.* — He-loves neighbor-ACC like soul-ACC-his. → "He loves his neighbor as himself."

"Each other" is *αλληλιμ*, which declines like a plural O-stem (ACC *αλληλονας*).

## Verbs

### Future: particle *ϸα* + present

> *ϸα νᾱϝω* — I will sail
> *ϸα νᾱϝει ναϝος* — The sailor will sail

### Narrative past prefix *ϝα-* (Phoenician wayyiqtol-analog)

In everyday storytelling and chant, sequential past events are marked with *ϝα-* prefixed to the present-tense form.

> *Ϝα-ϝενει ϝελος… ϝα-νᾱϝει ναϝᾱ… ϝα-λεκει βᾱσιλος.*
> "The wind came… the ship sailed… the captain spoke."

The form after *ϝα-* is the present (*ϝενει*, *νᾱϝει*, *λεκει*), as with the Hebrew *wayyiqtol*, which also uses the non-past form.

This form belongs to **oral tales, folk song and chant**. The [high register](registers.md#grammar), including written scripture and temple speech, tells the past with the aorist instead, and links the events with [participles](#participles): *ϝα-* there survives only in old fixed formulas. Older sacred narrative used *ϝα-* in writing, as Hebrew Bible prose uses the *wayyiqtol*, and the current [Mark 1](mark-1.md) is still in that style.

"To be" takes *ϝα-* on the Κ-Ν root, since the present root ε(σ)- takes no prefix: *ϝα-κανει* "and there was, and it came to pass", like Hebrew *wayhî*.

> *Ϝα-κανει ϙερεβον.* — "And it was evening."

### Participles

A participle is a verbal adjective. It agrees with its noun in gender, number and case, and declines like [*καλος*](#adjectives): ACC *νᾱϝοντον*, GEN *νᾱϝοντι*, plural *νᾱϝοντιμ*, LOC *νᾱϝοντελι*…

| | Active (AN / IN) | Middle (AN / IN) |
|--|------------------|------------------|
| **Present**: at the same time | *νᾱϝοντος* / *νᾱϝοντον*<br>nāvontos / nāvonton, "sailing" | *φερομενος* / *φερομενον*<br>pheromenos / pheromenon, "carried, being carried" |
| **Aorist**: before the main verb | *νᾱϝαντος* / *νᾱϝαντον*<br>nāvantos / nāvanton, "having sailed" | *φεραμενος* / *φεραμενον*<br>pheramenos / pheramenon, "having been carried" |

- The aorist participle is built on the aorist without its augment: *ενᾱϝα* → *νᾱϝαντος*; for Doric verbs *εποιησα* → *ποιησαντος*, *ευξαμην* → *ευξαμενος*. A vowel lengthened by the augment is short again: *ᾱρξαμην* → *αρξαμενος*.
- "To be" uses Κ-Ν: *κανοντος* "being", *καναντος* "having been".
- A participle is negated with *βαλ* before it: *βαλ νᾱϝοντος* "not sailing".
- Everyday speech uses participles mostly as plain adjectives and nouns: *νᾱϝοντος ναϝᾱ* "a sailing ship". The high register builds whole phrases with them, in place of relative clauses and chains of verbs ([Syntax § Participle phrases](syntax.md#participle-phrases)).

*Earlier versions gave the participle as νᾱϝον / νᾱϝουσα, the Greek masculine and feminine. Tharaic distinguishes animate and inanimate, not masculine and feminine, so the participle is now a regular adjective.*

---

### Imperfective past: *εκανα* + present

An ongoing or habitual action in the past uses the past of "to be" with the present form of the main verb. Both verbs agree with the subject. This is the same construction as Hebrew *hāyāh* + participle, and close to English "was sailing".

> *Εκανε νᾱϝει ναϝος ναϝᾱν.* — Was sails sailor ship. → "The sailor was sailing / used to sail the ship."
> *Εκανα νᾱϝω.* — "I was sailing."

It is negated as a whole: *βαλ εκανε νᾱϝει* "he was not sailing".

The high register uses the **present participle** in place of the second verb, as Greek and Hebrew can: *Εκανε ναϝος νᾱϝοντος.* "The sailor was sailing." The participle agrees with the subject.

### Middle voice

The middle voice shows that the action comes back to the subject: done **to oneself**, **for oneself**, or **to the subject by someone else** (the passive). It takes Greek-style endings.

**Present middle** of *φερ-* "carry":

| | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|--|----------|------|--------|
| 1st | *φερομαι*<br>pheromai | *φερεσθον*<br>pheresthon | *φερομεθα*<br>pherometha |
| 2nd | *φερεσαι*<br>pheresai | *φερεσθον*<br>pheresthon | *φερεσθε*<br>pheresthe |
| 3rd | *φερεται*<br>pheretai | *φερεσθον*<br>pheresthon | *φερονται*<br>pherontai |

**Aorist middle:**

| | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|--|----------|------|--------|
| 1st | *εφεραμην*<br>epheramēn | *εφερασθον*<br>epherasthon | *εφεραμεθα*<br>epherametha |
| 2nd | *εφερασο*<br>epheraso | *εφερασθον*<br>epherasthon | *εφερασθε*<br>epherasthe |
| 3rd | *εφερατο*<br>epherato | *εφερασθον*<br>epherasthon | *εφεραντο*<br>epheranto |

Infinitive: *φερεσθαι* (pheresthai). Imperative: *φερεσο!* (sg), *φερεσθε!* (pl), as in *Κεμεσο!* "Get up!" (*κεμεσθαι* "to rise"). The future is *ϸα* + present middle; the narrative past is *ϝα-* + present middle.

> *Ιδεται ναϝος ͱυδελι.* — Sees-himself sailor water-LOC. → "The sailor sees himself in the water."
> *Φερομαι φαρον.* — "I carry the cargo for myself."
> *Φερεται ναϝᾱ ϝελαθα.* — Is-carried ship wind-ABL. → "The ship is carried by the wind."

The agent of a passive goes in the **ablative**: the action comes *from* it.

### Doric verbs (high register)

The learned Doric verbs keep two Doric person endings: **1st plural *-μες*** and **3rd plural *-ντι***. Everything else is as for native verbs. Native verbs keep their own endings, even in high speech (*νᾱϝομεν*).

**Present** and **aorist** of *ποιειν* "to make, compose":

| | Present | Aorist |
|--|---------|--------|
| 1sg | *ποιω*<br>poiō | *εποιησα*<br>epoiēsa |
| 2sg | *ποιεις*<br>poieis | *εποιησας*<br>epoiēsas |
| 3sg | *ποιει*<br>poiei | *εποιησε*<br>epoiēse |
| dual | *ποιετον*<br>poieton | *εποιησατον*<br>epoiēsaton |
| 1pl | ***ποιομες***<br>poiomes | ***εποιησαμες***<br>epoiēsames |
| 2pl | *ποιετε*<br>poiete | *εποιησατε*<br>epoiēsate |
| 3pl | ***ποιοντι***<br>poionti | *εποιησαν*<br>epoiēsan |

- **The aorist cannot be predicted** from the infinitive, since it keeps the Greek aorist stem (*γραφειν* → *εγραψα*, *διδασκειν* → *εδιδαξα*, *ερχεσθαι* → *ηνθα*). The lexicon gives it for every Doric verb. All of them take the Tharaic aorist endings in *-α*, even Greek strong aorists: *εμαθα* "I learned" (Greek *ἔμαθον*).
- **The augment** is *ε-* before a consonant. A vowel is lengthened: **α → ᾱ** (the Doric long *ā*, where sailors' Greek would have *η*), ε → η, ο → ω, υ → ῡ. *ι* and the diphthongs stay as they are: *ᾱρξαμην* "I began", *ηλεησα* "I had mercy", *ͱῡμνησα* "I hymned", *ευξαμην* "I prayed". In verbs with a prefix, the augment goes after the prefix: *μετ-ε-νοησα*, *απ-ε-κριναμην*.
- **Middle verbs** have 1st plural *-μεσθα*: *ερχομεσθα* "we come", aorist *ευξαμεσθα* "we prayed". The other middle endings are the [native ones](#middle-voice).
- Future (*ϸα ποιω*), imperative (*ποιε!*), infinitive and participles are formed as for native verbs.

### "To be"

"To be" takes its forms from two roots. The present comes from a Greek-like root ε(σ)-; the past and the future come from the Phoenician-analog root **Κ-Ν** "to be, to stand firm".

| | Present | Aorist (past) | Future |
|--|---------|---------------|--------|
| 1sg | *εμι*<br>emi | *εκανα*<br>ekana | *ϸα κανω*<br>sha kanō |
| 2sg | *εις*<br>eis | *εκανας*<br>ekanas | *ϸα κανεις*<br>sha kaneis |
| 3sg | *εσι*<br>esi | *εκανε*<br>ekane | *ϸα κανει*<br>sha kanei |
| dual | *εστον*<br>eston | *εκανατον*<br>ekanaton | *ϸα κανετον*<br>sha kaneton |
| 1pl | *εσμεν*<br>esmen | *εκαναμεν*<br>ekanamen | *ϸα κανομεν*<br>sha kanomen |
| 2pl | *εστε*<br>este | *εκανατε*<br>ekanate | *ϸα κανετε*<br>sha kanete |
| 3pl | *εισι*<br>eisi | *εκαναν*<br>ekanan | *ϸα κανουσι*<br>sha kanousi |

Infinitive *κανειν* (kanein), imperative *κανε!* / *κανετε!* (kane! / kanete!). The present root has no infinitive or imperative of its own, so Κ-Ν fills the gap.

**Uses:**

- **Linking a subject and a description.** VSO order still holds: verb, subject, then the predicate, which agrees with the subject.
  > *Εσι θᾱρος καλος.* — "The sea is fair."
  > *Εσμεν ναϝιμ.* — "We are sailors."
  > *Εκανε βᾱσιλος ξενος.* — "The captain was a stranger."
  > *Ϸα κανω ναϝος.* — "I will be a sailor."
- **"There is".**
  > *Εσι ναϝᾱ εν λιμελι.* — "There is a ship in the harbor."
- **Possession**, with the owner in the dative. Tharaic has no verb "to have".
  > *Εσι ναϝᾱ βᾱσιλοι.* — Is ship captain-DAT. → "The captain has a ship."

### Comparison

| Degree | Ending (AN / IN) | Example |
|--------|------------------|---------|
| Comparative | *-οτερος* / *-οτερον*<br>-oteros / -oteron | *καλοτερος*<br>kaloteros "fairer, better" |
| Superlative | *-οτατος* / *-οτατον*<br>-otatos / -otaton | *καλοτατος*<br>kalotatos "fairest, best" |

The endings attach to the adjective's stem (*μεγ-ᾱς* → *μεγοτερος*) and decline like *καλος*. The standard of comparison goes in the ablative ([Syntax § Comparison](syntax.md#comparison)).

**Construct superlative.** Old titles and formulas use the Phoenician-style construct instead: a noun in the construct state followed by its own plural, as in Hebrew "song of songs". The high register uses *-οτατος*, except in the fixed title *ηλιλ ηλιμ* "god of gods".

> *ναϝιτ ναϝιμ* — "ship of ships", the finest ship
> *λακυλ λακα* — "word of words", the supreme word
